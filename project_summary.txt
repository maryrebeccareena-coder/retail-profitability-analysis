RETAIL PROFITABILITY ANALYSIS - PROJECT SUMMARY
Mary Rebecca Reena

PROJECT IN ONE LINE
Analysed 51,290 Global Superstore order lines (2011-2014) to find where the store loses money. Finding: no full-price order lost money; above a 20% discount almost every order does.

TOOLS
Python (Pandas, Google Colab), Google BigQuery (SQL), Power BI (Import mode), GitHub

DATA CLEANING NOTES
- Original: 51,290 rows, 24 columns
- Missing values: only Postal Code (41,296 rows, 80.5%) -> column dropped
- Duplicates found: 0
- Dates converted from dd-mm-yyyy
- Columns added: profit_margin, ship_days, order_year, order_month, season
- Final: 51,290 rows, 28 columns
- In BigQuery, a view (v_dashboard) adds discount_band and discount_band_order

TABLES (only sub-category with a loss)
Sales 757,042 | profit -64,083 | margin -8.5% | average discount 29.1%
None: +60,447 (23.1%) | 1-20%: +8,011 (5.0%) | 21-40%: -38,188 (-18.1%) | Above 40%: -94,353 (-76.7%)
467 lines above 20% discount lost 132,541; up to 20% it earned +68,458.

RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Cap discounts at 20%, manager approval above that
2. Stop deals above 40% first (98.4% lose money)
3. Review Tables pricing and discount policy

LIMITATIONS (say these in interviews, they show good judgement)
- No inventory data, so no inventory turnover or slow-mover analysis
- Discounts and losses go together; the data cannot prove the discount alone caused every loss
- The 815K figure is an upper bound, not a forecast
- Seasonality was not analysed in depth (season filter and monthly trend chart are on the dashboard)

2-MINUTE INTERVIEW PITCH (put this in your own words)
"I analysed 51,290 order lines from a global retailer to find out where it loses money. I cleaned the data in Python, ran the analysis in BigQuery with SQL, and built a Power BI dashboard. The store made 12.6 million in sales and 1.5 million in profit, but one in four order lines lost money. When I grouped orders by discount level, I found that no full-price order lost money, while above a 20% discount about 90% of orders did. Those orders lost around 815 thousand, more than half of the total profit. Tables was the only sub-category that lost money overall. My recommendation is to cap discounts at 20% with approval above that. A limitation is that the data has no inventory information, so I could not look at stock turnover."

INTERVIEW QUESTIONS TO PREPARE
- Why Import mode and not DirectQuery? Small static dataset; Import is faster and keeps working after the sandbox table expires.
- Why COUNTIF / SAFE_DIVIDE? COUNTIF counts rows meeting a condition; SAFE_DIVIDE avoids divide-by-zero errors.
- Why drop the postal code? 80.5% was empty, mostly outside the US.
- Why bands instead of raw discount? Easier to read and shows the 20% tipping point.
- What would you do with more data? Add cost and inventory data, test a discount cap on past orders, analyse seasonality.
- Does this prove discounts cause losses? No, it shows a strong association; costly items may also be discounted more.
